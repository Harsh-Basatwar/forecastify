# Forecastify — Shark Tank Pitch
### Pitch: 2 min 45 sec · then Q&A defence

> ⚠️ **Read this first.** Every number in **black** is real — pulled from your codebase, your deck, or public data. Every number in `[BRACKETS]` is one only you can fill: revenue, pilot stores, burn. **Do not invent them.** Sharks kill founders for made-up traction faster than for no traction. "We are pre-revenue with 4 pilot stores" is a survivable answer. A fabricated ₹40 lakh ARR is not.

---

## 🎯 THE ASK — decide this before anything else

**Recommended: ₹50 lakh for 4% equity (₹12.5 Cr valuation).**

Why that and not more: you are pre-revenue with a working product. Sharks price pre-revenue Indian SaaS at ₹8–15 Cr when the build is genuinely shipped. Ask ₹1 Cr for 2% (₹50 Cr) and you will spend the entire episode defending the valuation instead of selling the business — that is the single most common way a good product loses a deal.

**If you have crossed `[₹X lakh]` in revenue,** move to ₹75 lakh for 4% (₹18.75 Cr) and lead with the revenue number instead of the problem.

# 🎙️ THE PITCH

---

### ▸ THE OPENING (0:00 – 0:20)

> **[Walk in. Do not rush. Address the room, not the floor.]**

*"Hello Sharks. We are Team QuadCore — Aditya, Shubham, Harsh and Rohit — and we are here seeking **₹50 lakh for 4% equity** in our company, **Forecastify.***

*Sharks, before we tell you what we built, we want to ask you one question."*

---

### ▸ THE HOOK (0:20 – 1:00)

> **[Beat. Then:]**

*"How does the shop below your building decide what to order tomorrow?*

*The answer is: **he guesses.** He looks at what sold yesterday, he remembers last Diwali, and he guesses.*

*And that guess is expensive. When he guesses low, the customer walks to the shop across the road — and often never comes back. When he guesses high, the stock expires on his shelf.*

*Sharks, here is what that costs. **Twenty to thirty percent of annual revenue** lost to inventory mismanagement. **Fifteen to twenty percent** of perishable goods expiring before they are sold — roughly **₹50,000 per store, every year**, thrown in the bin.*

*Now multiply that by **thirteen million kirana stores** — the shops that still run **88% of India's grocery trade.***

> **[Slow down.]**

*Enterprise software solves this. It costs **₹5 to ₹10 lakh** to install. Not one of those thirteen million shops will ever buy it.*

*Amazon has a demand forecasting team. Reliance has a demand forecasting team.*

***The shop below your building has a notebook.***"

---

### ▸ THE PRODUCT (1:00 – 1:45)

> **[Turn to the screen. Show the live dashboard.]**

*"So we built **Forecastify** — an AI platform that predicts demand **per product, per store, per day**, and tells the shopkeeper exactly what to order and when.*

*This is not a slide. This is the live product, running on real sales data from a store in Jalgaon, Maharashtra. **Prediction accuracy: 81%.***

*Three things make it work, Sharks.*

***One — it watches the outside world, not just the till.*** *Weather, festivals, live news, inflation signals. Our Economic Impact Analyzer reads market conditions and adjusts stock recommendations automatically. **No competing retail tool in India does this.***

***Two — he doesn't have to type.*** *Meet JARVIS, our voice assistant — in **eight Indian languages**.*

> **[Speak into the mic. Let it answer out loud.]**
> **"JARVIS — is hafte kya stock mangana chahiye?"**

***Three — one click and it's ordered.*** *Our Chrome extension fills his cart on Udaan and IndiaMART automatically. Two hours of writing orders by hand becomes one click.*

*We built all of this in **56 hours**."*

---

### ▸ THE BUSINESS (1:45 – 2:20)

*"The model is simple SaaS. **₹499 per month** per store. Free tier to get them in, Pro at ₹499, custom pricing for retail chains.*

*Sharks, look at the maths from the shopkeeper's side, because that is the only maths that matters. He pays us **₹6,000 a year.** He is currently burning **₹50,000 a year in expired stock alone.** If we save him even **one-fifth** of that, he has made **₹10,000** on a ₹6,000 spend.*

***This product pays for itself the first time it prevents one crate of milk from expiring.***

*Market size: our serviceable market — smartphone-enabled kirana and mini-mart operators — is roughly **3 million stores**, or **₹1,800 crore a year**. We are targeting **30,000 stores in three years — ₹18 crore ARR** — which is **one percent** of that market.*

*Current status, honestly: `[we are pre-revenue with X pilot stores]` / `[we have done ₹X lakh in revenue with X paying stores]`."*

---

### ▸ THE ASK & CLOSE (2:20 – 2:45)

*"We are asking for **₹50 lakh for 4%.** That money does one thing: **distribution.** Getting a kirana owner to trust software is a feet-on-the-street problem, not a coding problem — and that is exactly where we need a Shark, not just a cheque.*

> **[Pause. Then, slower, straight to the room:]**

*Sharks, thirteen million shopkeepers in this country already know their customers better than any algorithm ever will.*

*What they have never had is **someone to tell them what's coming.***

***We are not digitising his ledger. We are giving him a forecast.***

*So — which Shark is coming with us?"*

# 🦈 SHARK Q&A — THE HARD QUESTIONS

The pitch is 3 minutes. The deal is won or lost in the next 15. These are the questions you **will** get, ranked by how likely they are to end you.

---

### 🔴 Q1. "Yeh toh koi bhi bana lega. Jio kal ye launch kar dega. What's your moat?"
> *The single most dangerous question. Never answer "our technology."*

**Answer:**
*"Sir, our moat is not the model — it's the data and the last mile.*

*One: every store that uses us makes the forecast better for the next store in that pincode. Our Federated Intelligence layer shares demand signals across nearby stores without exposing anyone's sales data. That is a compounding advantage a new entrant cannot buy.*

*Two: Jio and Amazon want the kirana as a **distribution channel** — they want him to buy from them. We are the only player whose incentive is to make him buy **less and better**. He can tell the difference.*

*Three: we work on Udaan and IndiaMART — everyone's supplier. A Reliance product will only ever fill a Reliance cart."*

---

### 🔴 Q2. "₹499 ek kirana wala kabhi nahi dega. He doesn't even pay for GST software."

**Answer:**
*"You're right that he won't pay for software. He will pay for **stock that doesn't rot.***

*That's why we don't sell software — we sell the saving. Our pitch to him is not 'AI dashboard'. It's: 'aapka ₹50,000 ka maal har saal kharab hota hai. ₹499 do, hum bachayenge.'*

*And that's why the free tier exists. He starts free, he watches our forecast beat his guess for three weeks, and then ₹499 is not a cost — it's obvious."*

---

### 🔴 Q3. "How will you acquire 30,000 stores? What's your CAC?"
> *Honest answer required — this is where pre-revenue founders get caught.*

**Answer:**
*"Honestly, Sir — that's the number we're here to find out, and that's why we want a Shark and not a loan.*

*Our plan is distributor-led, not direct. One Udaan or Metro distributor touches 200–500 kirana stores. We onboard the distributor, he onboards the shops — because our tool makes his customers order more predictably, which is his interest too. That turns a 30,000-store problem into a 100-distributor problem.*

*Current CAC: `[₹X]` from `[X]` pilots — or, if pre-revenue: 'we have not spent on acquisition yet; our first 90 days with your money is exactly this experiment.'"*

---

### 🟠 Q4. "81% accuracy — 81% of what? Who validated it?"

**Answer:**
*"It's measured against actual sales on a 500-transaction dataset from our Jalgaon pilot store, across 30 products. We evaluate on standard forecasting metrics — MAPE, WAPE, pinball loss — not a made-up score.*

*And I'll be straight with you: 81% on one store is a promising signal, not proof. Proving it needs `[X]` stores across `[X]` categories, and that's what this round funds."*

---

### 🟠 Q5. "You built it in 56 hours. Is this a hackathon project or a company?"

**Answer:**
*"It started as a hackathon. What's running today is not a hackathon demo — 133 APIs, 94 screens, ten forecasting models, Kubernetes deployment, SonarQube in CI/CD.*

*The honest answer, Sir, is: the product is a company. The business isn't one yet. That's the gap we're asking you to close."*

---

### 🟠 Q6. "Four founders, all technical. Who sells?"

**Answer:**
*"That's a fair hit. All four of us are engineers. Which is exactly why our ask is distribution money and a distribution Shark — the first hire out of this round is a field sales lead who has already sold to kirana stores, not another developer."*

---

### 🟡 Q7. "What if he has no sales history? Your AI has nothing to learn from."

**Answer:**
*"Cold start — we hit that on day one. A brand-new store produced flat, useless forecasts. We solved it by seeding from category-level benchmarks: a new dairy-heavy kirana starts on the aggregate pattern for dairy kiranas in that region, then converges to its own within a few weeks."*

---

### 🟡 Q8. "Why should he trust a number your AI made up?"

**Answer:**
*"Because we show our work. Every forecast can be opened up — which factors drove it, by how much, and what would change it. We also stamp a SHA-256 provenance record on every prediction, so it's auditable.*

*A shopkeeper who doesn't trust the number won't act on it. So we treated trust as a feature and built it."*

---

### 🟡 Q9. "Valuation ₹12.5 crore? Pre-revenue? Zyada nahi hai?"

**Answer:**
*"Sir, we've priced it below what a pre-revenue SaaS with a shipped product usually asks, precisely because we don't want to argue valuation with you — we want a partner.*

*What you're buying isn't a slide deck. It's a working product with `[X]` pilots, in a ₹1,800 crore serviceable market, with four engineers who built it in 56 hours and haven't stopped since.*

*But we're open. What number works for you?"*

> **Negotiation floor:** do not go below **₹8 Cr valuation** (₹50L for 6.25%). Below that you are giving away too much of a company that is mostly still ahead of you. If two Sharks join, ₹75L for 8% split is a good landing.

---

### 🟡 Q10. "How do you make money later? ₹499 forever?"

**Answer:**
*"₹499 is the wedge, not the business. Once we know what 30,000 stores are about to order, three things open up: transaction fees on the procurement we route to distributors, a demand-data product for FMCG brands who currently have zero visibility below the distributor, and working-capital lending underwritten on real sales data instead of a paper ledger.*

*But we're not pitching you that today. Today we're pitching ₹499 and one crate of milk."*

# 📋 DELIVERY NOTES

**Three silences to actually take:**
1. After *"...he guesses."* — let it land.
2. The full JARVIS voice response. Never talk over your own demo.
3. Before *"We are not digitising his ledger."*

**Rules of the room:**
- **One person pitches.** The other three answer only their own domain when asked. Four people interrupting each other is the fastest way to look unfundable.
- **Never say "no competitor has this" twice.** Once, about the Economic Impact Analyzer, is confident. Twice is arrogant.
- **When you don't know a number, say "I don't know."** Then say what you'd do to find out. Sharks fund people who can say that.
- **Never negotiate against yourself.** After you state the ask, stop talking. Whoever speaks first after the ask, loses.

**Demo insurance:** record the JARVIS voice query and the cart auto-fill in advance. If the network drops: *"live network's fighting us — here's this morning's recording"* — same words, no panic, keep moving.

**The three numbers to memorise cold:** 13 million stores · ₹50,000 wasted per store per year · ₹499 a month. If you forget everything else, those three sell the business.
